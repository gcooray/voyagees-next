Having spent time organising private drivers and tours in Sri Lanka, I have come to realise that the quality of a travel experience often depends on details that travellers might not consider when making a booking. The driver, the vehicle, the itinerary, and even how well everyone communicates can make a significant difference to a holiday.

Sri Lanka is a relatively small island, but travelling between destinations can take longer than expected. From busy Colombo streets to winding roads in the hill country, each journey comes with different considerations.

If you are planning to hire a private driver in Sri Lanka, here are the things I recommend looking into before making a decision.

## 1. Agree on the Costs Before the Trip

Understanding the costs, agreeing on what is and isn't covered, and getting it in writing beforehand usually saves headaches at the end. You don't need an official letter: a WhatsApp message with all the details is fine. But ask for it as a single message, rather than a few messages scattered across the conversation.

As a guide, these are typical daily prices for a vehicle with a driver on Voyagees:

- **Tuk-tuk:** around USD 40 a day
- **Car:** around USD 50 to 75 a day
- **Van:** around USD 75 to 95 a day

Prices vary with the season, the vehicle and your itinerary, so check the [current prices for your dates](/search) before you book.

Next, make sure you understand what is and isn't included in the price. Things to check include:

- Fuel
- Highway tolls
- Parking fees
- The driver's meals and accommodation
- How many kilometres are included per day, and the price per extra kilometre

Most large hotels, and some smaller ones, provide accommodation for drivers. Even smaller hotels usually have places nearby that do. Experienced drivers know these places and what they cost, so discuss this in advance.

Once the costs are agreed, agree on the payment schedule. A small deposit, such as Rs 10,000, is normal to confirm the booking. After that, around 20% of the total is often paid at the start of the tour, with the rest broken down into daily payments.

Tipping is not mandatory, but your driver will definitely appreciate it. There is no set amount. For a short trip of 2 to 3 days, around Rs 3,000 a day is a good minimum; for longer trips, around Rs 2,000 a day is common.

### Commission stops

I have added a separate section for this, because it is one of the most common complaints I hear from tourists. A commission stop is when a driver receives part of the sale for bringing you to a particular shop or restaurant.

Spice gardens, gem and jewellery shops, souvenir stores and some restaurants are where these commission stops usually happen.

Tell your driver beforehand that you're not interested, and that you'll only stop for shopping if you ask. Most drivers will respect this and leave you alone.

## 2. Consider Language Skills and Communication

Communication is one of the easiest things to overlook when booking a private driver, yet it can affect almost every part of the journey.

This is especially true if you're not fluent in English. Finding a driver who speaks your language then becomes critical. This is exactly why we show the languages spoken on every driver profile on Voyagees, and why you can filter drivers by language when you [search](/search).

![A Voyagees driver profile showing the languages the driver speaks](/images/blog/driver-profile-languages.png)

A driver does not need to speak perfect English, but they should be able to communicate clearly about pickup times, destinations, travel arrangements and unexpected changes.

I recommend having a conversation before confirming your booking. It gives you a chance to see how comfortably you can communicate, and whether the driver understands your expectations.

It is also worth exchanging WhatsApp details and agreeing on how to handle airport pickups, flight delays and itinerary changes.

If you want detailed explanations of historical sites and cultural attractions, check whether the driver is qualified to provide guiding services. Being able to communicate in English does not automatically make someone a qualified tour guide.

## 3. Choose a Vehicle That Suits Your Trip

The right vehicle depends on your group size, your luggage, and how much time you expect to spend on the road.

A sedan might be perfectly adequate for a couple travelling light, whereas a family with several suitcases may need an SUV or a van. For longer journeys, air conditioning, comfortable seating and enough luggage space become particularly important.

Before booking, confirm the vehicle's make and model, the number of passengers it can take, the luggage space and its general condition. Families travelling with young children should also ask about suitable child seats.

One practical point: a vehicle that technically fits your group may not have enough room for everyone's luggage. It is worth checking this in advance rather than discovering the problem at the airport. On Voyagees, each driver profile lists the vehicle, the number of seats and how much luggage it takes.

**Choosing a tuk-tuk:** tuk-tuks (three-wheelers) are an adventurous way to explore Sri Lanka, but they are definitely not the most comfortable option. They are great fun for a short trip, but I would avoid them for an island-wide tour or any journey of more than 3 to 4 hours.

## 4. Verify the Driver's Credentials

I would never recommend choosing a driver on price alone without checking their credentials.

Ask to see a valid driving licence, and check whether the driver holds the appropriate authorisation for tourist transport. The [Sri Lanka Tourism Development Authority](https://www.sltda.gov.lk/) publishes official information on registered tourist drivers and guides.

If you are booking through an agency, check its registration details and reputation as well.

These checks are particularly important for a multi-day trip, where you will rely on the same driver and vehicle throughout your holiday.

## 5. Ask About Insurance and Safety

Before confirming a booking, check that the vehicle is properly insured for the service being provided, and that passenger cover is in place.

Also check that there are seat belts for everyone and that the vehicle is well maintained.

I recommend asking what happens if the vehicle breaks down or there is an emergency during the trip. A clear back-up plan is especially useful in areas where other transport may not be easy to find.

Where you can, plan long drives during daylight. Many rural roads are poorly lit, and it is safer and far more enjoyable to see the scenery.

## 6. Read Reviews Carefully

Customer reviews are useful, but I would look beyond the overall rating.

Pay attention to comments about punctuality, driving, communication, the cleanliness of the vehicle, and how the driver handled unexpected situations. Reviews describing a similar itinerary are particularly helpful.

For example, feedback from someone who completed a ten-day island tour may be more relevant to your plans than a review from someone who only booked an airport transfer.

Google reviews and Tripadvisor are good places to start. If you are booking independently, you can also ask the driver for references from previous customers.

## 7. Plan a Realistic Itinerary

One of the most common mistakes when planning a tour of Sri Lanka is underestimating travel times.

Distances on a map can be misleading. Plan on an average of 30 to 40 km an hour door to door. A relatively short journey through the hill country can take much longer than expected because of winding roads, traffic and the weather.

Before finalising your itinerary, discuss your route with the driver and allow enough time for sightseeing, meals and rest stops.

For example, the drive from [Kandy](/destinations/kandy) to [Ella](/destinations/ella) takes around 5 to 6 hours by road. Think about how much time you want to spend at each destination, rather than treating the journey simply as a transfer between two points.

A good itinerary balances the number of places you visit with the time you have to enjoy them. Adding more stops does not always make a better holiday.

## 8. Be Clear About the Driver's Role

A private driver and a tour guide do not necessarily provide the same service.

A driver mainly handles transport, while a qualified guide can give you much more detail about Sri Lanka's history, culture, wildlife and attractions.

Some travellers are happy to explore independently and simply need reliable transport. Others prefer a more informative experience, and may want to arrange a separate guide for particular sites.

Neither approach is better. The important thing is to understand what is included in your booking, and to make sure it matches your expectations.

## 9. Check the Booking Conditions and Flexibility

Even well-planned trips can change. Flights may be delayed, the weather may affect your sightseeing, or you may decide to spend longer somewhere you love.

Before paying a deposit, clarify the cancellation policy, refund conditions, payment schedule and how changes to your itinerary are handled.

It is also worth asking what happens if the driver becomes unavailable or the vehicle develops a problem. Keep written confirmation of the agreed price, itinerary, vehicle details and contact information.

Having everything in writing makes it much easier to resolve misunderstandings if something doesn't go to plan.

## Final Thoughts

Hiring a private driver in Sri Lanka can be a practical way to explore the island, particularly if you are visiting several destinations or travelling with family. It offers flexibility and convenience, although public transport may suit travellers on a tighter budget, or those who enjoy planning every journey themselves.

From my experience organising private drivers and tours, the most important things are reliability, communication, realistic planning, and clarity about what you are paying for.

Take the time to discuss your itinerary, ask questions and set clear expectations before booking. A little preparation prevents many common problems, and helps you make the most of your time in Sri Lanka.
